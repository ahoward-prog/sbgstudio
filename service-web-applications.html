What We Do

Web Applications

Most web applications don’t fail from technical problems. They fail from strategic ones. They solve problems users don’t have, optimize flows users don’t follow, and assume behavior that doesn’t exist. Building sites that work means understanding the difference.

Our Approach

Building Experiences, Not Just Websites

Technology changes constantly, but human behavior changes slowly.

We’ve been building websites since the early days of the internet. Understanding the disconnect between fast-moving technology and slow-moving human behavior is what separates websites that perform from websites that look good in portfolios.

Early on we chased every new framework and technology. Now we focus on timeless principles: clarity over cleverness, speed over features, and reducing friction at every step. These principles outlast any JavaScript framework.

Partnership Over Vendor Relationships

Most stakeholders don’t know what they actually need until they see it working.

There are plenty of development shops that will build whatever you spec. The problem: requirements gathered in conference rooms rarely survive contact with real users.

We work differently. We challenge assumptions early, build prototypes to test hypotheses, and push back when requested features hurt the user experience. This approach requires trust — it’s why our client relationships measure in decades.

The Four Questions That Matter

Technology doesn’t determine if a web application succeeds — these four questions do. Answer them wrong and perfect code won’t save you. Answer them right and everything else gets easier.

What are we building?
For whom?
Why will they use it?
How will they use it?

Why Wireframes Come First

Wireframes force you to think about the user’s journey before colors and fonts.

They help you understand what users need to accomplish and remove everything that doesn’t serve that goal. When we wireframe first, we catch structural problems early — when they’re easy to fix. Skip wireframes and those same problems surface after design and development, when fixing them means rework, delays, and frustration.

Design as Foundation, Not Decoration

A visual system developers can implement consistently.

Design informed by strategy establishes patterns users can learn once and apply everywhere, making complex applications feel intuitive. We approach design as the foundation for everything that follows: done well, it accelerates development and improves user experience; treated as decoration applied at the end, it creates technical debt and user confusion.

Case Study

Omegasonics

The leading manufacturer of industrial ultrasonic cleaning systems, supplying equipment to over 20 industries and replacing time-consuming, toxic-solvent hand-scrubbing with cleaner technology.

The Challenge

Omegasonics had amassed a tremendous library of content from years of building custom solutions with customers. Part of the challenge was putting those resources to work to cultivate interest while improving search performance.

What We Built

A tiered system of concise mega-menus and landing pages for each segment, organizing hundreds of pages into a coherent process visitors could consume at their own pace — built on WordPress and WooCommerce with WordFence security integration.

The Results

We provide ongoing support for new product content and refine search capabilities as algorithms evolve. Over 60% of new revenue can now be tracked directly to the website.
