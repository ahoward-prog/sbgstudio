What We Do

Startup Companies

There’s a difference between advising startups and being a founder. We’ve been on both sides: raising Series A and B rounds, scaling operations across markets, building teams from zero to hundreds, and navigating exits. That experience changes how you think about brand, positioning, and growth.

Our Approach

Building Toward Exit From Day One

The companies that commanded higher multiples built brand equity that translated to enterprise value.

Most founders don’t think about exit strategy until they’re ready to sell. By then, it’s too late to fix the problems that reduce valuation. This means different things at different stages: at seed, proof of concept; at Series A, proving you can scale; at Series B, unit economics; at exit, how much brand equity an acquirer can leverage.

Why Most Startups Fail to Raise

The difference between a $5M and $20M valuation is usually the story, not the product.

Investors see hundreds of pitches. Most fail for the same reason: founders can’t articulate why they’ll win, not why their product is better. The companies that raised successfully told a story about market timing, competitive advantage, and team capability — investors don’t fund better mousetraps.

The Challenge

68% of shoppers abandon purchases due to delivery friction for big items.

What We Built

As co-founder and Brand Director, we built the “Good Guys” driver model, scaled to 90+ cities, raised $40M across Series A and B, and landed contracts with 20 of the top retailers in the country. Exited at Series B.

What We Learned

Early stage proves the model works in one market. Series A proves multiple markets. Series B proves unit economics at scale — the marketing that works at each stage is completely different.

Kallassy’s Swing Magic

The Challenge

Golfers struggle to improve swing mechanics without professional instruction; most training aids are expensive or ineffective.

What We Built

Swing Magic launched through direct response infomercial marketing, selling over 750,000 units worldwide as the most successful new golf product of its era.

What We Learned

Licensed by golf instructor Hank Haney and endorsed by top pros, it became a category-defining product that proved infomercial marketing could work for sports training equipment.

Games2U

The Challenge

Parents wanted premium entertainment for kids’ parties and events without the hassle of venue booking and travel.

What We Built

As an early investor, we helped the company raise their first million and developed the complete marketing package: game concepts, website, graphics, operating system, and franchise sales materials.

What We Learned

Games2U appeared on Shark Tank and sold 70 franchises in year one, 160 in year two. The founder successfully sold the company, which continues operating under new management.
